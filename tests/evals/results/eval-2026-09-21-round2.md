## Outcome

**This round does not count toward the 1.0 gate, and should not.** It found a defect
serious enough to move two of the five scores by 24 and 10 points. A round that changes
the tool is a round that tested a different tool than the one it finished with; the
counter starts again from the next unchanged run.

- Reports the **maintainer** would send unedited: **0 of 5** — four are close, all need a
  sentence of framing. Up from round one, where one I would not have sent at all.
- Reports the **practitioner** would send unedited: _open_. Still the gate.

### The site sample was the point

Round one ran five technical and specification sites and its own finding was that five
sites of one kind tell you nothing about whether the tool generalises. This round ran a
SaaS site, a publisher, an ecommerce catalogue, a non-profit and a reference site — and
the spread appeared immediately. Schema went from 0 on every site in round one to a
range of 0 to 65 here. The headline findings stopped being interchangeable.

### Changes made as a result

1. **The normalizer discarded most of a page whenever the page had several
   `<article>` elements.** `_pick_root` took the first one. On eff.org — no `<main>`,
   thirteen article teasers — that was fifty characters of a single card, so the site
   was scored on 100 characters, discovered one link instead of eighty-eight, crawled
   two pages instead of eight, and was reported as 32/100 *Poor*. It is 56/100 *Fair*
   on the same eight pages now. MDN moved 47 to 57 for the same reason.
   `<article>` is the content root only when the page has exactly one.
   `normalizer_version` is 2, so every affected evidence hash moves, by design.
2. **A blocking finding on one page led the whole report.** MDN had a single page of
   eight carrying a `noindex`, worth 0.38 composite points, at number one, because
   blocking findings were exempt from the page-level severity cap. A blocker on the
   site still leads; a blocker on one page is a page to go and look at. The cap and the
   flag now live in one method, so the two cannot disagree.

### Still open, and now said twice

The tool has no notion of what a site is *for*. It applied a publisher's checklist to a
specification in round one and to a reference site in this one. Upstream detected a
business type and tailored its recommendations; this rewrite dropped that and has not
replaced it. It is the most-repeated item across both rounds of maintainer notes and
should be the first thing a practitioner is asked about.
