### Changes made as a result

Four defects, all found by running this rather than by the test suite.

1. **Findings were ranked by category points, not composite points.** Thirty points of
   schema (category weight 10) outranked twenty-five of citability (weight 25) while
   being worth 3.0 against 6.25. Findings now carry an `impact` on the 0-100 composite
   scale and are ranked by it.
2. **A one-page outlier produced a site-level finding.** llmstxt.org's
   `self_containment` scored 22.16/25 across the site - comfortably healthy - and one
   bad page still made it the number one recommendation, marked critical. Site findings
   now come from the rolled-up signal; page-level outliers are reported separately,
   capped at medium unless they block.
3. **`content.expertise` claimed pages said nothing about their author** when they said
   it in prose. Retitled *Authorship is not machine-readable*, which is what is actually
   measured.
4. **The report did not say what the score measures** anywhere a reader would see it.
   That is what makes the sqlite.org verdict indefensible. A caveat now sits on page one
   under the number, not in the appendix.

### Left alone deliberately

Whether a cheap medium-severity win should outrank an expensive high-severity one.
Ordering by value-per-effort was tried: it put *add a modified date* first on five sites
out of five. The arithmetic is defensible and the report reads like a checklist. That is
a taste call about what an audit is for, not a bug, and it is the practitioner's to
make - it is recorded here rather than settled by whoever last edited the sort.

### Known limitation this round exposed

The composite can rate a genuinely excellent, heavily-cited site as *Weak* because it
weights machine-readable metadata heavily. That is the tool working as designed - it
measures what a machine can read - but the design assumption deserves the practitioner's
opinion, because it is the difference between a useful audit and one that gets dismissed
on sight.
